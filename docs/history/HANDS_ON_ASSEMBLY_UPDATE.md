# Hands-on assembly and sourced marketplace update

Run **Start_Windows.bat** after extracting the entire ZIP. Existing saves are supported. Unaccepted older build requests adopt the new terms; already accepted prepaid contracts retain their original agreement.

## What changed

- ASUS PRIME H510M-E and ASRock A520M-HDV have two memory sockets. The B560/B550 boards have four. Each slot holds one DIMM, has its own latch, and contributes its actual capacity. A 2-stick kit unpacks into two separately fitted modules.
- Hold a stocked part in the right tray and drag it to the physical socket or mount. Release to fit; incorrect locations and occupied slots reject the drop. Press **R** while holding to rotate and align it. Right-drag orbits the PC; the wheel zooms.
- Select **Driver**, select the component type, then hold an individual gold screw to tighten it. Green screws can be loosened. Select **Paste** and click the bare CPU before placing the cooler. Open individual RAM latches before fitting/removing modules.
- Drag installed components back into the right tray to remove them. Remove their fasteners first. Board/CPU dependencies, DDR generation, occupied positions and budget limits are enforced.
- Fans occupy independent rear, front and top mounts, up to six on supported cases. Each needs its own mounting screws and cable connection. Three ordered fans are three retail units, three parcels and three usable installed fans.
- Each ordered retail unit arrives separately. The courier returns to the van for the next box. A memory kit remains one retail box containing its two DIMMs.
- Beginners describe games, office work or editing and trust you to choose the hardware. Experienced customers can name specifications. The bench displays the resulting memory and storage targets.
- A new customer's budget includes **$50 labour**. They pay the actual fitted parts cost plus $50 when they collect the finished PC. There is no customer deposit. Optional in-game supplier credit funds accepted commissions and is repaid automatically from collection payments; unused budget is never charged.
- Every currently listed marketplace product has actual product photographs, a specification viewer, seller, condition, quote date and a link to the exact retailer listing. Photos also appear in the menu. Photo proportions are retained across widescreen layouts.

## Pricing

65 sourced USD listings are included, with 58 new/in-stock offers purchasable at the recorded snapshot. Seven unavailable or used listings remain visible but cannot be ordered as new. These are the exact selected listing prices to the cent, captured on **26 October 2026**, before tax and shipping. They are not automatically refreshed, universal prices, MSRP or necessarily the lowest offers. Some discontinued products have expensive third-party listings.

Unverified older product identities remain in saves and existing PCs but are not offered for new marketplace orders. See **PRICE_REFERENCE.md** and **assets/retail_catalog.json** for all source URLs, SKUs, specifications and original photo URLs. The white digital cooler uses the marketplace-listed manufacturer Fervit; it is not advertised as a verified Thermalright listing.

## Example

A $2000 request allows $1950 of parts. A tested white gaming configuration costs $1896.07; the customer pays $1946.07 at collection. If supplier credit funded all of those parts, repayment leaves exactly $50 in labour earnings.

## Verification and scope

The automated checks cover every product photo decoding, all nine commission budgets, independent DIMMs and fans, wrong sockets, occupied mounts, kit accounting, separate parcels, fractional prices, credit, collection payment, save/reload and ordinary repair RAM replacement. Renderer checks use real pointer presses and releases for CPU, fourth-DIMM and third-fan placement, plus held screwdriver input. Captures and results are included under verification/assembly.

This is still a simplified educational assembly simulation: parts snap after a valid manual drop; cable connections and the outer side panel use interface controls. The product photos show real hardware, while the in-world meshes remain original simplified models. Linux software-renderer checks do not establish Windows or RX 6600 FPS.
